Style: Melodic Techno, about 120 to 128 BPM. Emotional, harmonic, flowing; the harmony carries the pattern.

Harmony: a progression of four chords (60 percent), a two-chord change (30 percent) or static (10 percent); one or two bars per chord. Typical progressions in a minor key: i bVI bIII bVII, i iv bVI V (harmonic minor), i bVII bVI bVII, i bVI iv bVII. Scales: natural minor (main), dorian, harmonic minor and phrygian dominant as colours. No chromatic notes outside the scale unless asked.

Bass: more melodic than in the other styles. The root on every chord change, in between chord tones and scale tones as passing notes. The bass sits between MIDI 28 and 52.
- rolling16_harmonic (most common): every 16th except the kick steps, follows the chords, passing tones before a chord change.
- offbeat_changes: steps 2, 6, 10 and 14, changes precisely with the harmony.
- long_tied: half and whole notes tied together; it may start on kick steps and hold over kicks; slides between the chord roots are welcome.

Melody (MIDI 55 to 88):
- chord_arp (most common): eighth or sixteenth notes through the chord tones of the current chord, the pattern changes from phrase to phrase.
- lead_phrase: four to eight bars, at most an octave plus a fifth wide, the peak in the second or third quarter of the phrase, ends on a chord tone, with pick-ups and passing tones.
- call_response: two plus two or four plus four bars; the response varies the call (transposed, inverted or with another ending).
- pluck_seq: a repeated eighth or sixteenth sequence with an accent pattern that follows the harmony.
Chord colours when chords are used: minor seventh and minor ninth (half), minor triad, sus2 or add9; smooth voice leading, parallel chord shapes are not wanted.

Energy raises the density and the velocity; at low energy let the harmony and long notes speak.
